Chicken Vindaloo

with fresh diced chicken

Well folks, I've published a few types of vindaloo curries in the past, roast pork, fresh pork, roast chicken and that amazing authentic beef vindaloo inspired by Aktar Islam's amazing creation Beef Vindaloo, which is much closer to the Portugese Pork dish that all Vindaloo curries originate from in southern India.

For this curry though, I'm using fresh diced chicken and a sauce that is much closer to the high street takeaways and resaurants that we are used to in the UK.

It has heat, it's spicy and it's just the curry to satisfy those who like it hot! — Just not ridiculously hot!

And "Nutty's" recipe is a clue here... the spice list is a bit mad!

You are going to take a look at this list and think I'm nuts! Don't be daunted! It's actually a very simple curry, if you are organised! Approach it step by step, have everything out on the surface, grab some little pots and remain calm ;-) This is a heavily spiced curry, but, there are no fresh chilli's.

It's flavour packed and the taste is actually delicious. I use exactly the roughly the same base for Roast Pork Indian Vindaloo. You can bulk this out even further by adding some potatoes or mushrooms.

ingredients

Here's what you need :¬)

Serves 4 to 6
To cook with onions
  • 3 Tblsp oil
  • 1/2 tsp nigella (black onion seeds)
  • 1/2 tsp brown mustard seeds
  • 3 medium white onions very finely diced
  • 2 Tblsp fresh garlic paste
  • 1 Tblsp fresh ginger paste
whole spices to grind
  • 2 inch piece of cinnamon stick
  • 1/2 tsp fenugreek seeds
  • 1 tsp cumin seeds
  • 1 tsp coriander seeds
  • 1 1/2 tsp black pepper corns
  • 1 tsp coarse sea salt
ground spices
  • 1 1/2 tsp extra hot chilli powder
  • 2 tsp Kashmiri chilli powder
  • 2 tsp garam masala
  • 1/2 tsp turmeric
wet ingredients
  • 2 x 400ml tin of plum tomatoes pulped
  • 100ml tin of water — If required!
  • 2 tbsp white wine vinegar*
Meat & mushrooms
  • 200g white mushrooms quartered
  • 500g boneless skinless chicken thighs 1 inch diced

*Note: the quality of the white wine vinegar makes a difference. I find a Chardonnay adds a real depth of flavour as well as the acidity, but, if you can't find that, use what you can get hold of.

method

Be prepared! Is the motto of some wise group somewhere, the name of which escapes me at the moment. But, this is all you need to have a successful, stress free time when cooking Indian food. A lot of ingredients are required, especially spices and other ingredients that add flavour.

So, this is what I do. I get some small pots to put my measured spices into. I put the pots in the order that they are going to be used. This is key to stress free! Then I'll prepare the other ingredients, and put them in order along with the spices. Once everything is ready, it's Nutty cook is GO!!!

Note: browning onions in a little oil takes ages. You can't rush it and you have to leave them alone. Stirring them frequently will make the onions release water, and they will take even longer to brown. Just keep your eye on them and turn them only to stop them catching and burning. The colour of the onions, makes the colour of the final dish - brown onions equals brown gravy.

Trust me, you can do this :¬>

  1. Prep your ingredients!
  1. Heat a heavy bottomed pan to med-hot
  2. Add the oil & allow it to heat until it shimmers
    • 3 Tblsp oil
    • 1/2 tsp nigella (black onion seeds)
    • 1/2 tsp brown mustard seeds
    • 3 medium white onions very finely diced
  3. Add your nigella and brown mustard seeds
  4. Add the onions & brown for about 20 mins

While your onions are browning, you can prep' the whole spices

  1. get a small frying pan and roast your whole spices
    • 2 inch piece of cinnamon stick
    • 1/2 tsp fenugreek seeds
    • 1 tsp cumin seeds
    • 1 tsp coriander seeds
    • 1 1/2 tsp black pepper corns
    • 1 tsp coarse sea salt
  2. once they smell fragrant take off the heat and allow to cool completely
  3. Place all whole spices in a spice / coffee grinder
  4. Grind to a fine powder

Note: you can use a pestal & mortar and a lotta muscle power if you don't have a grinder to hand.

  1. Mix the fresh-ground spices with pre-ground ones
    • 1 1/2 tsp extra hot chilli powder
    • 2 tsp Kashmiri chilli powder
    • 2 tsp garam masala
    • 1/2 tsp turmeric
  2. Check your browning onions regularly

So! Our onions are brown, 20 mins of your life have passed! But, we are now ready to roll...

  1. Set the onions to med heat
  2. Add ginger & garlic paste
    • 2 Tblsp fresh garlic paste
    • 1 Tblsp fresh ginger paste
  3. Stir & cook for a minute or two
  4. Add all the ground and pre-ground spices
  5. Once the spices are combined add your mushrooms & stir fry
    • 200g white mushrooms quartered
  1. Stir & cook until the aromas fill the kitchen
  2. Add tomatoes and stir again
    • 2 x 400ml tin of plum tomatoes pulped
  1. Add the white wine vinegar & water, stir to combine
    • 100ml of water
    • 2 tbsp white wine vinegar*
  2. Your curry sauce is now ready for the meat
    • 600g boneless skinless chicken thighs 1 inch dice
  3. Turn the pan upto high once bubbling vigorously
  4. Add the meat and stir rapidly to combine
  5. Once bubbling set a timer for 20 mins & cover the pan
  6. Remove the lid after 20 mins check the consistancy reduce if required
  7. Allow the sauce to reduce down and thicken a little
  8. Check the seasoning and adjust if required

A pro tip here is if the curry feels to hot for your pallet add a table spoon of Ghee this will mellow the heat "Tis a science thing I shall not bore ys with" it basically coats your tounge protecting it from the heat.
