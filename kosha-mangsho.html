Kosha Mangsho

Wow! Are you in for a treat with this slow-cooked Kosha Mangsho made with lamb shanks.

This is a deeply savoury, heavily spiced curry with plenty of bang, starting with the distinctive flavour and aroma of mustard oil.

WARNING! If you've never cooked with mustard oil before, switch on your extractor fan and open the kitchen windows. Heating mustard oil will fill your kitchen with quite an eye-watering aroma, reminiscent of English mustard on steroids. Be prepared!!!

Traditionally made for special occasions, this dish deserves bread at the very least. But I like to go the extra leg and pair it with my Basanti Pulao rice for a restaurant-quality dining experience at home. Trust me, it's worth the effort!

The lamb marinates overnight, then simmers gently for three to five hours until the meat falls from the bone and the flavours have time to develop. As the marrow fat melts from the bones, it adds richness and a beautiful sheen to the masala, while the onions help give it body. The result is a rich, thick, deep-brown masala that clings to every morsel of tender lamb, with a beautiful glossy finish.

The good news? Once you've sorted out your spices, the preparation is fairly straightforward. It's just a lengthy recipe — so don't panic!

I made my own chilli paste (because I had a glut of fresh chillies to use) and garam masala, but you can buy both ready-made if you prefer.

ingredients

Here's what you need :¬)

serves 4

for the Marinade

  • 4 lamb shanks (approximately 1.4 kg in total)
  • ¾ cup plain yogurt
  • 1 Tbsp garlic paste
  • 1 Tbsp ginger paste
  • ½ tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp salt

For the cooking process

to start

  • 60 ml mustard oil*
  • 2-3 medium onions (about 400g in total)
  • ½ tsp salt

*No other oil will do! Make sure you buy food-grade mustard oil, too. I've noticed that some shops stock mustard oil intended for external use alongside their cooking oils.

Whole spices

  • 6 green cardamom
  • 6 cloves
  • 8-10 black peppercorns
  • 1-2 black cardamom
  • 1 stick cinnamon
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 2 dried red chillies

Ground spices

  • 1½ tsp cumin
  • 1½ coriander
  • ¾ tsp turmeric
  • 1½ tsp Kashmiri red chilli
  • ½ tsp jaggery or sugar

Wet and fresh ingredients

  • 1 Tbsp garlic chopped
  • 1 Tbsp ginger chopped
  • 4 green chillies
  • 4-5 Tbsp red chilli paste*
  • 500 ml water

* To make my own paste, I simply minced about 16–20 fresh red chillies with a little mustard oil. I had a glut of chillies from the garden to use up!

HEAT ALERT! This is a deliberately punchy dish and, if you're concerned about the final heat level (it is hot!), you can reduce the amount of fresh chillies and chilli paste. But it's never too hot for Nutty!

Garam masala*

  • 5 green cardamom
  • 1 inch cinnamon
  • 1 black cardamom
  • 5 cloves
  • 10 black peppercorns
  • ¼ tsp nutmeg
  • 1 mace flower
  • ⅓ star anise
  • Pinch of salt

* You only need a teaspoon of the garam masala, so if you wish to use your usual one, I shall not judge you! But for authenticity, I'll be making my own. Why? Well, simply because I'm Nutty...

method

Time! You'll need plenty of it for this dish. First, marinate the lamb overnight (12 hours). Then allow about 20 minutes to organise your spices and grind them where necessary. Cooking the onions and spices takes another 20–30 minutes before the lamb goes on to slow-cook for three to five hours.

So, realistically, you're looking at 17–18 hours from start to finish, including the overnight marinade — although you can get a little shut-eye while the lamb marinates!

Okay! First things first, you'll need to get the lamb shanks into the marinade and leave them overnight.

NB: You'll notice that I use three pans, one to cook the spices in, one to brown the lamb shanks in and finally a differnt pan to for the slow-cook of the curry. If you have one pan that will do the job, just use that! The important point is that the lamb is fully submerged in the masala once the slow-cook starts — If you need to add more water to submerge the meat completely you will have to take the lid off the pan to reduce the liquid near the end of the cook, so that you obtain that rich, thick, glossy finish to the masala.

To make the curry, you'll need to get your spice ingredients ready then it'll be fairly straightforward.

We start off by infusing whole spices into mustard oil, softening onions for the base of the sauce and gradually adding spice ingredients as we go along.

Then we need to cook the meat slowly until it literally falls off the bone. We don't want to have to use a carving knife at the table — I'm just remembering one particular dining-out experience we had! Least said, soonest forgotten!

I'm making a specific garam masala to finish the curry, but you could use a ready-made mix from the shop. I won't judge you! I just like to make mine fresh and tailor it to the dish — it's definitely worth doing.

So, let's do this thing!

The Garam Masala

You can make this first or leave the job until the meat goes on to simmer.

  1. Put all the Garam Masala ingredients into a grinder.
    • 5 green cardamom
    • 1 inch cinnamon
    • 1 black cardamom
    • 5 cloves
    • 10 black peppercorns
    • ¼ tsp nutmeg
    • 1 mace flower
    • ⅓ star anise
    • Pinch of salt
  2. Grind to a fine powder.

That's it! Job done! Told you it was simple :-)

You'll have enough here to sprinkle on your rice too!

The marinade

Take a container large enough to hold the lamb shanks. I use a plastic one with a sealable lid.

  1. Mix all the marinade ingredients together to make the marinade.
    • ¾ cup plain yogurt
    • 1 Tbsp garlic paste
    • 1 Tbsp ginger paste
    • ½ tsp turmeric
    • 1 tsp salt
  2. Use all the prepared marinade to coat the lamb shanks thoroughly.
    • 1.4kg (approx) 4 Lamb shanks.
    • All of the prepared marinade to coat the lamb.
  3. Cover and refrigerate overnight (about 12 hours).

The Curry

I prep' my ingredients from the list and arrange them in the order that they are used — which should be the same order as the ingredients list if the editor has got it right!

So below I have the mustard oil and whole spices on the left and the garam masala for finishing on the right.

Take the ingredients listed under 'For The Cooking Process' and Whole Spices too.

I'm using three pans here, but if you have one that will do the job, just use that one! The most important point is the pan is deep enough to allow you to submerge the joints completely while they cook. If you need to add more water to cover the joints, you will need to reduce the liquid towards the end of the cooking process. i.e. take the lid off and turn the heat up!

  1. Heat the mustard oil in the wok until the aroma fills your kitchen
    • 60 ml mustard oil
  2. Fry the whole spices gently, just until they become fragrant:
    • 6 green cardamom
    • 6 cloves
    • 8-10 black peppercorns
    • 1-2 black cardamom
    • 1 stick cinnamon
    • 2 bay leaves
    • 2 dried red chillies

NB: Don't overcook the spices or they will become bitter and spoil the curry.

  1. Add the sliced onions & salt.
    • 2-3 medium onions (about 400g in total)
    • ½ tsp salt
  1. Coat the onions in the oil and cover to soften
  2. Turn the onions occasionally.

We want the onions to be pale golden brown, not a deep dark colour, so 15 to 20 mins should do.

  1. Add ginger & garlic:
    • 1 Tbsp garlic chopped
    • 1 Tbsp ginger chopped
  2. Cook till the raw aroma has gone.
  1. Add the chilli paste & sliced chillies:
    • 4 green chillies
    • 4-5 Tbsp red chilli paste
  2. Fry for 3-4 mins.
  1. Add the ground spices:
    • 1½ tsp cumin
    • 1½ coriander
    • ¾ tsp turmeric
    • 1½ tsp Kashmiri red chilli
    • ½ tsp jaggery or sugar
  2. Cook gently for 2-3 mins.

Now remove the lamb shanks from the marinade and seal the lamb all over in the fried onions and spices. Set the excess marinade aside while you seal the lamb.

I'm using my dutch oven to do this just as it has a nice wide base that the lamb shanks will fit in. However, it would take too much water to cover the lamb completely in this pan, so I'll have to transfer it to a smaller, deeper pan for the slow cook. More washing up I know!

  1. Seal the meat on all sides.
  2. Add the 500ml water & the reserved marinade.

Scrape the brown stuck bits off the bottom of the pan and transfer everything to a deep pan for the simmering.

It's best if the joints are completely submerged for the duration.

  1. Tip everything into a deep pan and submerge the joints completely*
  2. Add the remaining marinade that you set aside & mix well.
  3. Bring to a boil then turn the heat down to a simmer with the lid on.
  4. Simmer and stir occasionally for 3-5 hours until the meat is falling off the bone.

*If you need to add more water to cover the lamb completely, add more water! The finished sauce should be rich, thick and glossy. If it's still thin towards the end of the cooking time, remove the lid and let it reduce before serving.

Test for doneness. The lamb should be so tender that it falls off the bone easily, or at least a fork should easily slide into it.

If you can remove the lamb shanks from the sauce intact, it's great for the presentation, but don't sweat, I didn't manage it this time round!

Finally, sprinkle a teaspoon of garam masala onto the curry, cover and allow to warm for five minutes with the lid on before serving.

  1. Add 1 tsp garam masala.
  2. Cover for 5 mins.
  3. Stir well but gently before serving.

If you leave the finished dish in the fridge overnight and reheat it gently the next day, the resulting flavours will be exceptional. That's what I do with this curry and it is definitely worth doing!

Serving Suggestion: Boiled rice is just not good enough for this curry! It deserves bread at least and a special rice if you want to elevate the experience to a completely new level. For that, I prepared Basanti Pulao rice. It takes a little more love to prepare, but you will not be disappointed. Trust me on this!
